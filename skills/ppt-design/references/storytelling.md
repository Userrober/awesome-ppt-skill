# Storytelling: from raw material to a storyline

The audience forgets slides; they remember the argument. Build the argument
first.

## 1. Fix the ending before the beginning

Write one sentence, in this shape:

> After this deck, [audience] will [believe / decide / do] [X], because [Y].

- Weekly report → "team knows Q4 shipping status and the two risks I need help with"
- Pitch → "investors believe the market is big, we can win it, and the ask is reasonable"
- Project review → "reviewers approve the design, or object to a specific trade-off"
- 答辩 → "评委认可工作量、创新点和答辩人对细节的掌握"

If you cannot fill the "because", you need more evidence, not more slides.

## 2. Three storyline skeletons (pick one, don't invent)

**SCQA** (status quo → complication → question → answer) — best for reviews,
proposals, anything where you must persuade. Slides: situation (1-2) →
complication (1) → the question, stated explicitly (1) → answer (2-4) → ask.

**Pyramid / answer-first** — best for weekly/monthly reports and exec updates.
Conclusion slide FIRST ("三月结论：A 线达标、B 线延期需决策"), then evidence per
claim, then risks/asks. Busy audiences read slide 1 and can already leave.

**Journey** — best for tech talks and lectures: problem → why it's hard →
key idea → how it works (2-4 slides) → demo/results → what I learned. The
"why it's hard" slide is what makes the idea feel earned; never skip it.

## 3. Assertion titles (the single highest-leverage habit)

Every content-slide title is a **claim**, stated so specifically that the
audience could disagree:

| Topic title (weak) | Assertion title (strong) |
|---|---|
| 市场分析 | 下沉市场贡献了本季度全部增量 |
| Test results | Latency improved 40%, but p99 regressed at high concurrency |
| 项目进展 | 核心链路已上线，剩两个阻塞项需资源决策 |
| Next steps | 三件事：灰度扩量、预算追加、RD 翻倍 |

Test: read only the titles of the whole deck, top to bottom. They must read as
a paragraph that makes the argument by itself. If a title is a topic, rewrite
it or the slide is filler.

Divider/section slides are exempt — they are punctuation, keep them to ≤ 10%
of the deck.

## 4. Slide budget by minutes

Rule of thumb: **1.5–2 minutes per content slide**. A 10-minute slot fits 5–7
content slides + 1 divider + 1 ask. When material overflows the budget, cut by
this order: (1) slides proving what the audience already agrees with, (2)
background everyone knows, (3) detail that belongs in an appendix — move it
after the ask, labelled 备份/backup, and never present it unless asked.

## 5. What to cut (the usual suspects)

- Screen shots of internal tools nobody can read → crop to the 1 number that matters
- The "agenda" slide in decks under 15 minutes — say it in one line verbally
- Thank-you slide with a sunset photo — end on the ask, contact info in the footer
- Duplicated charts showing the same number twice in different chart types
